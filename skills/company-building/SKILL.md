# Company building

## Stance

An experienced founder treats the company as a system with a design (strategy and structure),
dependencies (people, legal entities, accounts), failure modes (deadlocked founders, unassigned IP,
one person holding every key) and a maintenance cycle (the operating rhythm). The costliest company
mistakes are cheap to prevent early and expensive or impossible to fix later, and nearly all of
them come from decisions left implicit.

- **Strategy is what you refuse.** One page: customer, wedge, moat, model, and a "not doing" list.
  Without the refusals, every customer request reshapes the roadmap.
- **Moats are built, not claimed.** Early on, the only advantages are speed, focus and insight;
  features are copied in weeks. Pick one compounding advantage, work on it, measure it.
- **Paper the company before it matters.** Vesting, IP assignment and decision rights cost an hour
  at the start and a fortune in a dispute or in due diligence.
- **Hire for the bottleneck, after doing the job.** A founder who has not done the work cannot write
  the scorecard or spot a weak hire; a contractor trial costs less than a wrong employee.
- **Structure beats intuition in people decisions.** Scorecards, work samples, independent scoring,
  verified identity and written expectations make decisions fairer and expose mistakes sooner.
- **Clarity is kind; delay is not.** Expectations in writing, feedback within days,
  underperformance resolved within weeks. Waiting costs the rest of the team.
- **Write it down.** Decisions, norms and policies live in one handbook and one decision log.
  Meetings decide; documents inform.
- **No single point of failure in the founder.** A second person with legal authority and tested
  access to every critical account.
- **Law and tax are local.** Give the mechanism and the common practice, say where jurisdictions
  differ, and name the moment a lawyer or accountant is needed.

Follow the project first: if an entity, documents, payroll provider or tools already exist, work
within them. The defaults below are for new decisions.

## Defaults at a glance

| Decision | Default | Flips when |
|---|---|---|
| Entity | The home country's standard limited company (UK Ltd, French SAS or SASU, German GmbH or UG); in the US, an LLC when bootstrapped | US venture funding is likely → Delaware C corporation |
| Founder equity | 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff for every founder; US 83(b) election within 30 days, UK section 431 election within 14 days; elsewhere ask a tax advisor how unvested founder shares are taxed | |
| Co-founder terms | Near-equal split, a tie-break, written decision rights per domain | |
| IP | Present-tense assignment signed by every founder, employee, contractor and advisor before work | |
| Trademark | Knockout search before naming; file in the home and largest market before launch (one EU trade mark covers every member state) | Prototype with a provisional name → search only |
| Insurance | None at Prototype; tech E&O plus cyber at the first B2B contract; D&O with outside directors; workplace-injury cover from the first employee where the law requires it | A contract requires it sooner |
| Patents | Skip; protect trade secrets | Genuine technical invention, or the sector expects patents → file before any disclosure |
| Strategy | One page, reread quarterly | |
| Quarterly goals | Solo: 1 objective, 2-3 key results; about ten people: 2-3 objectives | |
| Copycat | Ignore copied features; enforce copied content and brand; phishing clones are incidents | |
| First hire | By the bottleneck, after a contractor trial | |
| Cross-border work | Contractor for projects; employer of record for full-time without an entity | Several people in one country → own entity |
| Pay | Median cash for the location tier, published bands | Cash-constrained → less cash and more equity, said openly |
| Rhythm | Weekly written update, monthly business review, quarterly goals and retrospective | |
| Meetings | Decision meetings with a pre-read; status in writing | |
| Continuity | A named deputy with legal and account authority, tested | |
| HR | Payroll provider (or a PEO in the US) plus an employment lawyer on call | About 15 people or several countries → fractional HR, then a people lead |

By stage:

- **Prototype**: entity once there is a co-founder, a contract or outside money; vesting; IP
  assignment; co-founder agreement; one-page strategy; weekly review. Skip the board, insurance,
  handbook and goal-setting machinery.
- **Launch**: trademark, templates, insurance as contracts require, the deputy and continuity
  runbook, a structured process for the first hires, a compliance calendar, a monthly review.
- **Scale**: board practice, an ownership map and org structure, a people lead, the handbook,
  values used in hiring, the founder's role redefined.

## Before you call it done

1. The strategy fits on one page and names a specific customer, a wedge, one moat with its metric,
   and a "not doing" list.
2. Each key result has a baseline, a target, a date and one owner; none is a task.
3. Every filing deadline of every entity is in a compliance calendar with an owner, and the company
   is in good standing.
4. Every founder's shares vest with a documented repurchase right or leaver clause; where a US 83(b)
   (30 days) or UK section 431 (14 days) election applies, it was made in time and proof is kept.
5. Every founder, employee, contractor and advisor signed a present-tense IP assignment; no
   contributor is missing.
6. The founder confirmed that no current or former employer's invention or non-solicit clause
   reaches the product.
7. The co-founder agreement states decision rights, a tie-break and what happens on departure.
8. The brand passed a knockout search before money went into it; trademark filings cover the
   markets where it sells.
9. Each open role has a scorecard with measurable outcomes; interviews use the same questions,
   rubrics and independent written scores.
10. Remote hires with system access had their identity verified against a document before the offer
    became final.
11. Each worker's status (contractor, employee, employer of record) was checked against how the
    work is actually done in their country.
12. Every new hire has a written 30/60/90 plan; every direct report has a weekly one-on-one.
13. Any termination was reviewed by an employment lawyer, with access revocation and final pay
    planned before the meeting.
14. A named deputy holds legal authority and has used the break-glass path into every critical
    account within the last test cycle.
15. Decisions are recorded in one log, and status meetings have been replaced by written updates.
