# Running a fundraise

Read this when: preparing a pitch deck or data room, choosing between a SAFE, a note and a priced
round, reading or negotiating a term sheet, checking out an investor, running the investor
pipeline, or closing a round.

## Before starting

- Start when this round's milestones are met (`funding-strategy.md`) and at least six to nine
  months of real runway remain. A raise commonly takes three to six months from first meeting to
  money and can fail; raising with three months left hands investors the terms.
- Books closed and reconciled, MRR definitions written, cap table current. Diligence finds every
  gap, and fixing them mid-raise kills momentum.
- One founder runs the raise; the other runs the company. Metrics sliding during the raise are a
  common reason late conversations die.

## Pitch deck

| Slide | Must answer | Investors check |
|---|---|---|
| Title and one-liner | What it is, for whom | Whether they can repeat it |
| Problem | Who hurts, how much, today | A specific customer, not a category |
| Product | How it works; screenshots or a short demo | That it exists and is used |
| Traction | Revenue or usage over time, retention, notable customers | That numbers match the data room |
| Why now | What changed | That timing is real |
| Market | Bottom-up: number of target customers × realistic price | Top-down "1% of" math is a red flag |
| Business model | Pricing, gross margin, unit economics | Margin math |
| Go-to-market | How customers are found, with evidence from the channel that works | A working channel, not a list |
| Competition | Honest alternatives, including spreadsheets and doing nothing | "No competition" signals naivety |
| Team | Why these people win this | Relevant experience, full-time commitment |
| Round and use of funds | Amount, the milestones it buys, runway | That the milestones match the next round's bar |

- Two versions: a send-ahead deck that reads without a presenter, and a presenting deck with
  less text. Ten to fifteen slides; put traction early when it is the strongest point.
- Every number traces to the books or the billing export. A deck number that differs from the
  data room ends conversations.
- Share as a tracked link rather than an attachment, so access can be revoked and interest seen.

## Data room for a round

Open it when an investor is past first meetings and asks for diligence, not before; a data room
sent with the first email is read by nobody and leaks.

For a seed or Series A: incorporation and governing documents with all board and stockholder
consents; fully diluted cap table with the list of SAFEs and notes; monthly financial statements
for the last 24 months or since inception; MRR export with the bridge and a cohort retention
table; the 18-month model; top customer contracts; IP assignments signed by every founder,
employee and contractor; key vendor and partner contracts; privacy policy, terms and a security
overview; employee list with roles and start dates. Build it as a subset of the standing data
room in `exit-due-diligence.md`.

## Instruments

| Instrument | What it is | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Post-money SAFE (the US standard form) | A right to future shares at a valuation cap and/or discount; no interest, no maturity | Pre-seed and seed in the US; fast, cheap, closes investor by investor |
| Convertible note | Debt converting at the next round; interest and a maturity date | Investors or jurisdictions that expect it; the maturity date becomes investor leverage, so set it long |
| Priced round (Series Seed or full venture documents) | Sale of preferred shares at a set price, with board and investor rights | Larger rounds, a lead who wants governance, or after SAFEs have stacked up |
| UK advance subscription agreement | Prepaid subscription converting into shares, structured to keep SEIS and EIS eligibility | UK angel rounds relying on those reliefs; a US-style SAFE does not qualify |
| Other jurisdictions | Local equivalents, for example convertible loans in Germany, BSA AIR in France | Use the local standard; local investors know it |

## SAFE and priced-round arithmetic

Post-money SAFE: ownership at conversion = investment ÷ post-money cap, measured against the
capitalization just before the priced round. Each additional SAFE dilutes the founders, not the
earlier SAFE holders.

| SAFE | Investment | Post-money cap | Ownership at conversion |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | 500k | 5M | 10% |
| Second | 1M | 10M | 10% |
| Together | 1.5M | | 20%, before the priced round dilutes everyone |

Founders who sign several SAFEs without modeling them regularly discover they sold far more than
intended. Model each one in the cap table before signing (`equity-cap-table.md`).

- A discount SAFE converts at the priced round's price minus the discount; with both a cap and
  a discount, the holder gets whichever yields more shares.
- An MFN (most favored nation) SAFE takes the best terms of later SAFEs.
- Pro rata rights usually come by side letter; give them to investors who will follow on.

Priced round:

- Post-money = pre-money + investment; new investors' share = investment ÷ post-money.
- **Option pool shuffle**: investors usually require the pool to reach a target size after the
  round, created inside the pre-money, so it dilutes only existing holders. Example: 2M on an 8M
  pre-money buys 20%; adding a 10% post-money pool inside the pre-money makes the founders'
  effective pre-money 7M, and existing holders keep 70%, not 80%. Size the pool from a hiring
  plan to the next round, not a round number.

## Term sheet: the terms that matter

| Term | Common at seed and Series A | Push back on |
|---|---|---|
| Amount and valuation | Pre- or post-money stated explicitly | Ambiguity about which; a pool in the pre-money with no hiring plan behind it |
| Liquidation preference | 1× non-participating: investors take their money back or convert, whichever is more | Participating preferred, multiples above 1×, cumulative dividends |
| Anti-dilution | Broad-based weighted average | Full ratchet |
| Board | Seed: founders keep the board. Series A: often two founders and one investor, or five seats with an independent | Investor majority at an early round |
| Protective provisions | Preferred consent for changes to their rights, new senior shares, a sale or liquidation, debt above a limit | Operational vetoes: budget approval, hiring, pricing |
| Pro rata | Right to keep ownership in later rounds | Super pro rata for small checks |
| Information rights | Annual and quarterly financials for major investors | Monthly audited anything |
| Founder vesting | Existing vesting continues, with credit for time served | A fresh four-year restart for founders already vested |
| Drag-along | Needs the board and a majority of common as well as preferred | Preferred alone can force a sale |
| Redemption | None | A right to demand repayment after a period |
| No-shop | A few weeks | Long exclusivity with no commitment to close |
| Legal fees | Company pays investor counsel up to a cap | Uncapped |

Waterfall, a 20M sale, investors put in 10M for 25%:

- 1× non-participating: investors take the greater of 10M or 25% of 20M (5M), so 10M; common
  gets 10M.
- 1× participating: investors take 10M plus 25% of the remaining 10M, so 12.5M; common gets 7.5M.

Use the standard model documents (NVCA in the US, BVCA in the UK, the standard SAFE) and founder
counsel who does venture financings every week. Deviations from the model forms are where the
cost hides; ask for a redline against the standard.

## Vetting investors

An investor stays on the cap table for the company's life and may sit on the board. Check them as
carefully as they check the company.

- Ask for references, then find the ones not offered: founders of portfolio companies that
  struggled, took a down round, replaced a founder or shut down. The test is behavior when
  things go badly.
- Check the person, not the brand: the partner who will sit on the board or be the contact.
- Ask: did they do what they promised before the money? How did they behave in a bridge or down
  round? Did they push for or against a sale? How fast did they answer in a crisis? Would you take
  their money again?
- Check the fund's position: a fund late in its life has little reserve for follow-on and may
  push for an early exit.
- Red flags: an exploding term sheet with a deadline of days; re-trading after signing; heavy
  control terms at seed; slow decisions on small things; claims of help that founders do not
  confirm.

## Running the process

1. **Target list** of investors matching stage, check size, sector and lead or follow role, with
   no competing portfolio company. Tier it A, B, C. Track in a CRM or sheet: stage, next step,
   date, notes, reasons for passing.
2. **Warm introductions**, best from founders they backed, via a short forwardable email with the
   deck link. Cold email works with a sharp, specific note, at a lower rate.
3. **Practice** in a few C-tier conversations first; fix the story from their questions.
4. **Compress**: book A and B first meetings within two to three weeks so interest arrives in
   parallel. Parallel timelines create urgency; a sequential raise lets every investor wait for
   the others.
5. **Follow up within a day** with what was asked; send short notes to the pipeline when a
   milestone lands.
6. **Find the lead**: the lead sets terms and others follow. "Interested, come back when you
   have a lead" is not a commitment.
7. **Timebox**: if several weeks of active meetings bring no lead, stop, take the feedback, and
   fix the business or the story before burning the rest of the list.

Slow replies and requests for "more traction" mean no; anything short of a signed term sheet is
uncommitted. Do not advertise the raise publicly while it runs: in the US, general solicitation
changes which securities exemption applies and what investor verification is needed.

## Negotiating

- Leverage comes from alternatives: a competing term sheet, or enough runway to walk away.
- Spend negotiating capital on a few things: amount, dilution including the pool, board control,
  clean terms. A higher headline valuation with structure attached is usually worse than a lower
  one on standard terms.
- Valuation is not the goal: an inflated price raises the bar for the next round and makes a
  down round more likely.
- Exploding offers: ask for the time needed to hear from the others in process; a good investor
  grants it.
- SAFEs: negotiate the cap and pro rata; keep the standard form unmodified so later investors do
  not need to re-read it.
- Have founder counsel mark up the term sheet before signing. It is mostly non-binding, but it
  fixes every later document, and its no-shop and confidentiality clauses do bind.

## Closing the round

For a priced round (a SAFE collapses to signing and wiring):

1. Term sheet signed; the no-shop starts.
2. Confirmatory diligence; a disclosure schedule listing every exception to the company's
   representations.
3. Definitive documents (in the US standard set: stock purchase agreement, amended charter,
   investor rights, voting, and right-of-first-refusal and co-sale agreements), usually drafted
   by investor counsel and reviewed by company counsel.
4. Board and stockholder approvals; in Delaware the amended certificate of incorporation is filed
   before the closing.
5. Signatures, then wires. Confirm wire instructions by phone to a known number: changed bank
   details by email are the standard fraud.
6. Shares issued or SAFEs recorded; cap table updated the same day.
7. Post-closing filings. US: Form D with the SEC within 15 days of the first sale under
   Regulation D, plus state notice filings. UK: return of allotment to Companies House within one
   month, and SEIS or EIS compliance statements so investors can claim relief.
8. A new 409A valuation before the next option grants (`equity-cap-table.md`).
9. Announce only after the money is in the bank.

Take money only from accredited or otherwise qualified investors unless counsel has prepared the
disclosure that others require.

## Ask the user

- **How much is needed, and what dilution is acceptable?** Default: 18 to 24 months to the next
  milestones, aiming to sell no more than about a quarter of the company.
- **Which SAFEs or notes exist already, with caps and discounts?** They change every dilution
  number. Default: none assumed; ask for the documents.
- **How much does board control matter to the founders?** Default: keep a founder majority
  through the seed.
- **Is a fast close or the best terms more important?** Default: best terms, unless real runway
  is under six months.
- **Where are the company and the investors, and do tax reliefs (SEIS, EIS, qualified small
  business stock) matter to them?** Default: ask before choosing the instrument.
