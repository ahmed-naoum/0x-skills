# Content marketing

Read this when: deciding whether content should be a channel, planning what to publish, editing
AI-drafted articles, building in public, using docs or free tools to acquire users, repurposing
content, or judging whether content produces customers.

## Decide whether content is the channel

Content compounds slowly and pays for a long time. It fits when:

| Condition | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Buyers search for or discuss the problem in public | Content needs existing questions to answer |
| The founder knows something the reader does not | Generic content has no audience left to win |
| The product can be shown inside the answer | Content that cannot lead to the product produces readers, not users |
| The company can wait months for results | Search-driven content takes months to rank and convert; as a rule of thumb, plan on two quarters before judging it |

When revenue is needed within weeks, content is the second channel, not the first: outreach,
communities or a launch are faster (choosing channels: `paid-acquisition.md`).

Start where intent is highest, because it converts with little traffic:

| Intent | Examples | Converts | Start at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision | "[Competitor] alternatives", "[Product] vs [Competitor]", "best [category] for [segment]" | Highest | Launch |
| Solution | "How to [job the product does]", integration guides, templates | High when the product is the method | Launch |
| Problem | Why the problem happens, benchmarks, guides to the domain | Medium; earns trust and links | Once the first two are covered |
| Thought leadership | Opinions, trends, founder essays | Low directly; builds audience | Ongoing, through building in public |

## The content engine

A repeatable pipeline beats bursts of inspiration. Each stage has an output:

1. **Collect questions** into one backlog, verbatim, with their source: support tickets, sales
   and demo calls, onboarding answers, community threads where the target customer asks,
   search-console queries the site already appears for, complaints in reviews of competitors.
2. **Score** each one: product fit (does the answer naturally use the product), buyer intent,
   and whether the company can say something others cannot. Search volume is a tiebreaker, not
   the driver (keyword research and search demand: `seo-aeo-geo-ultimate`).
3. **Brief**: the reader and their situation; the question; the answer in one sentence; the
   first-hand material it will contain (data, screenshots, a customer example, the founder's
   opinion); the product's role; the call to action; sources to verify.
4. **Draft**, from the founder's own explanation where possible (next section).
5. **Expert edit** to the quality bar below.
6. **Publish** with a real author, the date, and a CTA matched to the intent (template, free
   tool, trial).
7. **Distribute**: newsletter, the founder's social accounts, the communities where the question
   was asked (answer there, link if allowed), partner newsletters. Rule of thumb: spend about as
   long distributing a piece as writing it.
8. **Measure and refresh**: review each piece's signups after a few months; update the winners,
   merge or remove pieces that bring nothing.

Cadence: one strong piece a week or every two weeks, kept up for a year, beats daily posts for a
month. Batch the work (a research session, a drafting session) instead of starting from zero
for each piece.

## Editing AI-drafted content

An unedited model draft reads like every other unedited model draft, and readers and search
engines discount it. The draft is raw material; the value is added in the edit.

The best source material: the founder explains the topic aloud for fifteen minutes (or answers
the brief's questions), the recording is transcribed, and the model drafts from the transcript.
The draft then carries real expertise and real opinions, and editing becomes tightening.

Edit pass, in this order:

1. **Answer first.** The first paragraph answers the question; cut throat-clearing intros.
2. **Delete the generic.** For each paragraph, ask whether a competitor's article would contain
   it unchanged. If yes, make it specific or remove it.
3. **Add first-hand material**: a real number, a screenshot of the actual result, a customer
   example (with permission), what the founder tried that failed, an opinion with its reason.
4. **Verify every fact, statistic, quote and link** against a primary source. Models produce
   plausible statistics and citations that do not exist; one invented number found by a reader
   discredits the whole site.
5. **Remove model tics**: hedged non-statements, stock openers and closers, triplets of
   adjectives, a conclusion that repeats the article, uniform paragraph lengths.
6. **Run every code sample and command** shown.
7. **Byline a real person** with relevant experience who stands behind the piece.

Search engines judge content by quality, not by how it was produced, but Google's spam policies
treat pages mass-produced mainly to rank as scaled content abuse, which can demote a whole site.
Never generate pages in bulk (one per keyword or city) without unique, useful substance on each;
template-driven pages are their own discipline (`seo-aeo-geo-ultimate`).

Use the model for research summaries, outlines, drafts from transcripts, repurposing and
copyediting. Never let it supply facts, data, customer stories or opinions the founder does not
hold.

## Proprietary expertise and data

Content competitors cannot copy is the only content that keeps working once anyone can generate
articles.

| Source | Example | Care needed |
|---|---|---|
| Aggregate product data | Median time to an outcome across accounts; a yearly benchmark report | Aggregate and anonymize, set a minimum group size, confirm terms and privacy policy allow it |
| Customer results | Case studies, before-and-after numbers | Written permission |
| Original surveys | A survey of the target audience, published with its method | Describe the sample honestly; no overclaiming |
| The founder's experience | What was tried, what failed, the real numbers | Decide upfront what is public |
| Teardowns and tests | Hands-on comparisons with screenshots | Fair, dated, reproducible |
| Expert interviews | Practitioners answering the reader's question | Their approval of quotes |

Original data is what other writers cite and link to, which carries it far beyond the site's own
audience. Publish the method with the numbers so the claim survives scrutiny.

## Building in public

Consistent founder-led posting turns a solo company's weakness (no brand) into an advantage (a
person people follow and trust).

- **One primary platform**, where the customers are: developers and indie founders on X,
  Bluesky and Hacker News; B2B buyers on LinkedIn; visual or how-to products on YouTube or short
  video. Cross-post elsewhere, adapted.
- **A sustainable cadence for months**: several posts a week is a common rhythm. A missed week
  matters less than a habit that stops.
- **The mix**: mostly the customer's problem domain, then product progress with screenshots,
  decisions with their reasoning, numbers when comfortable, failures and lessons.
- **The trap**: posting about the startup journey attracts other founders, who rarely buy.
  Unless the customers are founders, talk mainly about the customer's problem and show the
  product solving it.
- **Limits decided upfront**: no customer data, no revenue figures unless the founder is
  comfortable, nothing a competitor could exploit before it ships.
- **Convert followers into owned contacts**: every few posts, point to the newsletter, the
  waitlist or a free tool. Followers belong to the platform; the list belongs to the company.

## Docs, examples and tools as acquisition

For developer and technical products, documentation is marketing: evaluators read the docs
before the homepage, and docs pages answer the searches developers actually make.

- Public and indexable; never behind a login.
- A quickstart that reaches a working result in minutes, with copy-paste commands.
- Example repositories and starter templates for the popular frameworks, each a landing page.
- Integration guides titled by the job ("Send [X] to [popular tool]").
- Pages for error messages and common problems: developers search the exact error text.
- Measure docs visits that precede signups and the quickstart completion rate.

Writing and maintaining docs: `0x:code-quality`. A public API's developer platform:
`0x:api-design`.

## Free tools, templates and calculators

A free tool meets the target customer at the moment of the problem and demonstrates the
product's value instead of describing it.

- **Adjacent to the paid job**: it solves a nearby problem for the same person (a pricing
  calculator for a billing product, a header checker for a security product). A popular tool
  for the wrong audience brings traffic that never converts.
- **Solves something in one session** and produces a result worth saving or sharing.
- **Does not gate the basic result.** Gating kills use and sharing. Gate the extras: saving,
  exporting a report, monitoring over time, team access.
- **Puts the CTA at the moment of value**, beside the result, connecting it to the paid product.
- **Is cheap to run**: client-side where possible. If it calls paid APIs (an LLM, a data
  provider), add rate limits and bot protection before announcing it, or it becomes a cost leak
  (`0x:auth-identity` for abuse defense).
- Templates and checklists are the cheapest version; a template that opens inside the product
  is the strongest, because it is also the first step of activation.
- Downloadable ebooks and generic whitepapers are the weakest lead magnet for a small company.

## Repurposing one piece

Produce one substantial piece, then adapt it natively to each channel instead of writing ten
unrelated ones.

| From one pillar article or recording | Adapted into |
|---|---|
| The core argument | A thread or LinkedIn post with one visual |
| The data or chart | A standalone image post |
| The walkthrough | A short captioned screen recording |
| The full piece | A newsletter issue with a personal introduction |
| The questions it answers | Answers in communities where those questions are asked |
| The spoken version | A webinar, podcast appearance or talk |

Change the opening and format for each platform; one link pasted unchanged everywhere
underperforms. Reuse what already worked: a piece that performed well deserves a new format,
not retirement.

## Measuring by signups and pipeline

Pageviews say nothing about whether content brings customers. Track per piece:

| Metric | How |
|---|---|
| Signups with the piece as first touch | First-touch landing page stored on the account at signup |
| Signups that read the piece before signing up | Content views in the visitor's history before signup |
| Activated and paying users among those | Join to activation and billing data |
| Self-reported source | "How did you hear about us?" free text at signup or onboarding |
| Pipeline influenced (sales-assisted products) | Content viewed by accounts with open deals |
| Leading indicators | Search impressions and clicks for target queries, newsletter signups |

- Judge a piece after it has had time to rank and be shared, typically a few months.
- Compare signups per visit across pieces; the outliers show which intents and formats to make
  more of.
- Self-reported attribution catches what tracking misses (podcasts, word of mouth, AI-assistant
  answers). Keep it free text and code the answers monthly.

Attribution models and their traps: `0x:product-analytics`.

## Ask the user

- **How long can content run before it must show signups?** Default: two quarters for
  search-driven content, with weekly signals from community distribution meanwhile.
- **Who is the public author?** Default: the founder by name; a faceless brand earns less trust.
- **Which data may be published?** Default: aggregate, anonymized product data where the terms
  allow; no revenue figures without explicit approval.
- **How many hours a week are available for content?** Default: plan the cadence on half the
  hoped-for hours; a sustainable pace matters more than volume.
- **Which platform will the founder show up on consistently?** Default: the one where customers
  already read and discuss the problem.
