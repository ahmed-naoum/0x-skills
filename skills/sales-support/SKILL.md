# Sales, support and customer success

## Stance

For a solo founder, selling and supporting are one job at two moments: learning what a specific
customer needs, then making sure they get it. An expert here is unsentimental about time
(disqualifies fast, writes everything down, puts a date on every step) and generous where
generosity is cheap (fast replies, easy refunds, closing the loop). Most of the work is
sequencing and follow-through, not persuasion.

- **The founder sells and supports first.** Calls and tickets are where the ideal customer,
  the price, the objections and the product gaps are discovered; delegating before the process
  repeats hands over a guess.
- **Diagnose before prescribing.** Discovery before the demo, reproduction before the answer,
  the churn cause before the save offer. The wrong pitch, fix or offer costs more than the question.
- **Every open item has an owner and a date.** Deals without a dated next step, tickets without a
  promised time and renewals without a reminder are how revenue leaks without anyone noticing.
- **Trade, never give.** Discounts, terms and custom work are exchanged for commitment: prepay,
  term, a reference, a signature date. A concession given for nothing becomes the floor.
- **Promise only what is true and can be operated.** Questionnaire answers, roadmap dates, SLAs
  and contract clauses become obligations; "not yet, planned for Q3" beats a false yes.
- **Cheap generosity beats expensive friction.** A quick small refund, cancellation in the product
  and a fast human answer cost less than a chargeback, a public complaint or a lost account.
- **Separate the kinds before acting.** Involuntary or voluntary churn; bug, gap or confusion;
  blocker or preference. Each has a different owner and fix.
- **Close the loop.** Telling a customer their bug is fixed, their feature shipped, or their
  introduction became a customer is the highest-return habit in this area.
- **Write it down so it can be handed over.** CRM notes, the answer library, policies and the
  playbook are all the first hire will inherit.

Follow the project first: if a CRM, help desk, billing provider or admin tool exists, work in it.
The defaults below are for new decisions.

## Defaults at a glance

| Decision | Default | Flips when |
|---|---|---|
| First customers | Warm introductions, double opt-in, from the founder's network | Network exhausted → narrow cold outbound |
| Pipeline tool | Spreadsheet | About ten open deals or a second seller → lightweight hosted CRM |
| Pipeline stages | Defined by buyer actions, each deal with a dated next step | Never by seller activity |
| Cold email | Separate sending domain, plain text, no tracking, three to five touches in two to three weeks | Recipient country requires consent → no cold email there |
| Qualification | Pain, budget, authority, timeline | Deal involves procurement → MEDDICC |
| Pilot | None; a monthly plan is the trial | Large deal needing proof → paid, 30 to 60 days, written criteria and conversion terms |
| Contract | Order form on standard terms, e-signature | Customer's paper only above a set deal size |
| Liability | Mutual cap at 12 months' fees; supercap only for data and confidentiality | Counsel advises otherwise for the deal |
| Payment | Annual prepay, net 30, card or bank transfer | Strategic logo → longer terms, traded for something |
| Security material | Overview, policies, pentest letter, pre-filled CAIQ before the first enterprise deal | SOC 2 once its absence blocks material revenue |
| Onboarding | Founder onboards every account | After about twenty accounts → bands by contract value |
| Support | Help desk, email plus in-app form, normal tickets answered within one business day | Live chat only when staffed |
| Refunds | Written public policy; support may refund up to one month alone | Above that, founder approves |
| AI support agent | Not before the top articles exist; a human on request, always | |
| Admin access | Read-only, logged, time-limited impersonation | Write access only with customer consent |
| Health score | Three to five signals, red, yellow, green, in a query plus a CRM field | CS platform at Scale |
| Cancellation | In the product, required reason, one matched offer, pause option | |
| Reviews | Ask everyone at a defined success moment; no gating, no incentives tied to sentiment | |

By stage:

- **Prototype**: warm introductions, problem conversations, a spreadsheet, the founder answering
  every message personally. Skip CRMs, sequences, help desks, health scores and playbooks.
- **Launch**: CRM with enforced stages, separate cold-email domain, standard terms and order form,
  help desk with published targets and refund policy, top-ten articles, onboarding checklist,
  cancellation flow, security pack before the first enterprise deal, contract renewal reminders.
- **Scale**: playbook, first rep with a written comp plan, first support and success hires, health
  score validated against past churn, business reviews, reference program, redline playbook.

## Before you call it done

1. Every open deal in the CRM has a stage that matches its exit criterion, an amount, a dated next
   step and a close date; none has a next step in the past.
2. Cold email goes from a separate domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC aligned, without tracking, with
   an opt-out, and a shared suppression list stops every sequence on reply, bounce or opt-out.
3. Every qualified deal has a named economic buyer, a quantified pain and a known buying process;
   unknowns are written as questions for the next call.
4. A pilot, if any, has written success criteria agreed with the economic buyer and conversion
   terms signed before it starts.
5. Every concession in a contract was traded for something and is recorded with the contract.
6. Each signed contract is stored with its terms version, and its renewal and notice-deadline
   reminders exist; non-standard obligations have an owner.
7. Every questionnaire answer and trust-pack document is true today and verified by someone who
   knows the system.
8. New accounts have a defined first-value event and a milestone tracker; stalled accounts
   generate a task automatically.
9. Support has published hours and targets, an auto-acknowledgment, and articles for the top ten
   questions linked from saved replies.
10. The refund and cancellation policy is written, public and applied without case-by-case
    approval below the stated limit; cancellation works in the product.
11. Every admin and impersonation action is permission-gated and lands in an append-only audit
    log with actor, target, reason and ticket.
12. Tickets close with a root-cause tag, and linked customers are notified when their bug is
    fixed or feature ships.
13. Churned accounts are split into involuntary and voluntary, each with a recorded cause.
14. Public replies to reviews or complaints reveal no customer details and make no unreviewed
    admission of legal liability.
15. Customer names, logos and quotes are used only with written permission.
